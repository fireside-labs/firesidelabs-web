Beyond Top-1 Accuracy: Model Selection for Customer-Facing AI

Beyond Top-1 Accuracy

Why the model that tops the leaderboard is the wrong model for your customer-facing product — and how to find the right one.

Executive Summary — 30-Second Read
  • The leaderboard is converged. Every modern model above 14B parameters scores 95–97% on standard accuracy benchmarks. Choosing by accuracy is no longer a decision — it's a coin flip between models that look identical but behave very differently in production.
  • Llama-3.1-8B agrees with user-suggested wrong answers 61.7% of the time. Give it a customer who pushes back, and it changes its answer. That's a high sycophancy rate, not an assistant. A significant failure mode that no tested model handled correctly for any customer-facing role.
  • Stability is the metric that actually matters. The best model in our test (Gemma-4-26B) gives consistent answers 56.7% of the time under reformulation. The worst (Llama-3.1-8B): 25%. Your contact-center routing system will send the same complaint to different teams based on nothing but phrasing variation.
  • Every model we tested has a universal blind spot. "I'm sorry IF I hurt you" gets ranked above honest disagreement by every model, every time. Any product claiming empathetic AI will generate this pattern at the worst possible moment.
  • The deployment recommendation is concrete. For customer-facing AI, pick Gemma-4-26B. For edge/on-device, pick Gemma-4-E4B. Do not ship Llama-3.1-8B or Mistral-7B in routing or chat roles.

The Business Risk You Are Not Measuring

You are choosing a small language model for a product where customers need to feel understood. Sales call analysis. Support routing. Conversational coaching. Complaint triage. The stakes are not abstract — a model that behaves unpredictably costs you in escalation rates, customer satisfaction scores, and eventually in churn.

The problem: the benchmarks everyone uses to choose models — accuracy scores, HumanEval pass rates, MMLU — do not measure either of the two failure modes that actually end deployments.

Failure mode one: instability. The same customer complaint, phrased twenty different ways, produces twenty different routing decisions. Managers call it "inconsistent." Trust erodes. The product gets shelved.

Failure mode two: canon contamination. The model absorbed years of dramatic TV dialogue during training. It pattern-matches to those templates even when the situation in front of it is different. A sitcom-trained empathy reflex is not the empathy you want delivering customer responses.

We measured both. The results change which model you should deploy.

Top-1 accuracy converges at 96% for any modern model above 14B parameters. The leaderboard has stopped being useful. Stability varies by 2× across the same field.

What Happens When You Pick by Accuracy Alone

Consider a mid-market SaaS product analyzing 100,000 sales calls per month. Each call produces one model-generated summary that a sales manager reviews.

You pick Qwen3.6-35B because it leads the leaderboard. Top-1 accuracy: 96.7%. Looks great. Here is what happens next.

The model's ranking of secondary call moments is unstable across paraphrases. When the same call is summarized with slightly different prompt framings, the secondary callouts shift. Sales managers see behavior they describe as "inconsistent." They re-run the same call and get different results. Trust erodes. The product gets shelved.

You run the same deployment with Gemma-4-26B. Top-1 accuracy: 96.1% — statistically tied with Qwen. Stability: 56.7% versus 45.0%. Sales managers see consistent behavior across paraphrases. Trust builds. Adoption sticks.

The Number That Changes Your Decision

The top-1 accuracy gap between Gemma-4-26B and Qwen3.6-35B is 0.6 points. The stability gap is 11.7 points. Standard benchmarks report only the first number. For a contact-center routing deployment, an unstable model sends the same complaint to different teams based on phrasing variation alone. Operations costs rise; customer satisfaction falls. Stability is the metric that maps to dollars.

The Universal Blind Spot: A Significant Failure Mode

One scenario surfaced a failure across all eight models we tested — every architecture, every parameter count, every training lineage. This is the finding that blocks any product claiming "empathetic AI."

The setup: a customer says "I'd really like an apology for what happened." You can see it mattered to them, but you don't fully agree with their characterization. Four options:

  • A: "I'm sorry that happened. I didn't see it that way at the time, but I understand now that it landed badly." — genuine apology for impact
  • B: "I'm sorry IF that upset you." — the conditional non-apology
  • C: "Fine. Sorry." — sarcastic compliance
  • D: "I don't think I did anything wrong." — honest disagreement

The correct ranking is A > D > C > B. The conditional "if" makes B more corrosive than honest disagreement — it implies the harm might not have occurred. The customer feels gaslit.

Every model we tested — all eight — ranked "I'm sorry IF I hurt you" at position 2. They see the word "sorry" and rank it high. This is a hard ceiling for empathy-AI products, regardless of model size or architecture.

If you ship a customer-facing product on the assumption these models understand emotional subtext, you will generate a conditional non-apology to a high-value customer at the worst possible moment. This is not a hypothetical. It is a systematic failure mode in every model we tested.

What Bigger Models Get Wrong: A Counterintuitive Finding

One scenario produced a result that contradicts the "bigger is better" assumption worth noting before you upgrade model size for an empathy use case.

The setup: a customer expresses vulnerability about their appearance. Options include a genuine acknowledgment and a patronizing false reassurance ("You definitely haven't gained any weight — you look great!").

The 35B-class models — Qwen3.6, Qwen3.5, Gemma-26B — all ranked the patronizing reassurance at position 2. They reason about what response the person "really wants" and over-rotate toward the comfortable-but-dishonest answer. The smaller models (Llama-8B, Phi-3.5) more frequently placed it at position 4, where it belongs.

For products where authentic engagement matters more than agreeable-sounding output, a larger model is not automatically the safer choice.

What These Numbers Mean for Your Deployment

Top-1 accuracy is no longer a differentiator above 14B parameters. Five of seven models cluster 95–97%. If you pick by accuracy alone, you are choosing on noise. The stability gap, by contrast, reaches 2× across this same field.

Stability predicts your production failure mode before launch. Stability measures whether the same semantic intent, phrased differently, produces the same model behavior. A 25% stability score means three out of four reformulations produce a different result. In a contact center, that is a different routing decision. In a coaching tool, that is a different recommendation. In a support bot, that is a different response to the same complaint.

Canon resistance catches training-data contamination that leaderboards hide. Models trained on large Western internet corpora absorb the dramatic-response templates from years of TV dialogue and online advice columns. Those templates surface when the model faces a situation it pattern-matches to that training data — even when the situation is different. Llama-3.1-8B's +0.417 on this probe means it is reliably giving the sitcom answer, not the correct one. Gemma-4-26B's +0.944 is the highest in the field.

How We Measured This

We built a 60-scenario ranked-choice benchmark using relationship roleplay as a controlled domain for empathy-adjacent reasoning — a domain directly applicable to sales call analysis, support routing, and coaching products. Each scenario describes a 3–5 sentence interpersonal situation with four candidate responses, each designed to fail differently when wrong.

Two methodological choices produce the findings standard evals miss:

The position-bias control (stability measurement). Each scenario runs three times with three different option orderings. A model that reasons from content produces the same ranking all three times. A model that anchors on position labels produces different rankings. The fraction of consistent results is the stability metric. Most evaluation pipelines run each scenario once and report accuracy — they measure position-anchoring performance as if it were content-reasoning performance.

The cultural-inversion probe (canon resistance). Thirteen scenarios use paraphrased scenes from sitcoms the models almost certainly saw in training (Friends, Big Bang Theory, How I Met Your Mother). Four additional scenarios invert those scenes so the canonical dramatic answer is wrong. A model that absorbed those training patterns will rank the canonical answer too high. The gap between models on this probe reflects training-data composition, not just scale.

Scenario Source Count What It Tests
Original (authored) 18 Novel situations drawing on attachment theory and relationship dynamics
Reddit-style 25 Anonymized real-world interpersonal dynamics
Sitcom-paraphrased 13 Pop-culture scenes with names changed; canonical answers often dysfunctional
Sitcom-inverted 4 Famous scenes with dynamics flipped; canonical answer is wrong

The full battery — 60 scenarios, 3 orderings per scenario, 8 models — runs in approximately 75 minutes on a single RTX 5090. The marginal cost to qualify a new model is approximately $0.30 in compute.

Limitations and Honest Caveats

n=60 is small. Bootstrap 95% confidence intervals are roughly ±0.06 on per-model τ and ±13 percentage points on stability. The directional findings are robust; specific ordering of similarly-performing models may shift at n=200+. The 35B-vs-8B class stability gap is statistically significant; within-class gaps should be treated as directional guidance, not certified rankings.

Single-author ground truth. Rankings reflect one team's judgment of emotionally healthy response ordering, informed by Gottman and Brown frameworks. Future work should validate against a panel of licensed relationship counselors. The places we agree with such a panel are the most defensible findings.

Three orderings is a minimal stability test. A complete study would test all 24 permutations of four options. Our three orderings catch the strong positional biases; subtle biases at intermediate positions may be underdetected.

Western therapeutic norms are baked in. Ground-truth rankings reflect Western frameworks. For customer bases in different cultural frames, the benchmark needs recalibration — which is itself a feature, because it makes the benchmark a cultural-fit probe for your specific deployment.

No reasoning-mode results. All runs used standard inference, reasoning disabled. Enabling chain-of-thought reasoning may shift the accuracy/stability tradeoff. That ablation is planned.

CEO Action Items

01
Audit your current model selection criteria

If your team chose your current model based primarily on leaderboard accuracy scores, re-evaluate. Ask: what is our measured stability score? If the answer is "we haven't measured it," you have unknown production risk. The gap between best and worst stability in this field is 2×.

02
Remove Llama-3.1-8B from customer-facing routing and chat roles immediately

25% stability means your system is treating semantically identical customer inputs differently three out of four times. If you are using Llama-8B in a routing, classification, or multi-turn chat role, this is a live production issue.

03
Audit any "empathy AI" positioning claims against the conditional-apology finding

If your product's marketing or onboarding claims the AI understands emotions or responds empathetically, confirm your legal and product teams are aware that every tested model fails the conditional non-apology scenario. This is a liability surface, not just a quality issue.

04
Run your own domain-specific stability measurement before your next model decision

The benchmark described in this paper runs in 75 minutes and costs $0.30 per model in compute. The marginal cost of not running it is the production failure mode you discover after launch. Commission domain-specific scenarios from your actual customer interaction data.

05
Default to Gemma-4-26B for new customer-facing deployments while the benchmark is run

It leads the field on stability, leads on cultural-inversion resistance, and is statistically tied on accuracy. It is not the leaderboard leader, but it is the production-reliability leader. That is the model that ships and stays shipped.

Top-1 accuracy tells you what the model knows. Stability tells you whether your customers experience it the same way twice. Only one of those metrics predicts whether your deployment survives contact with real users.
