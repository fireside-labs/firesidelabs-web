The Fireside Chimera Beats Both Parents

The Fireside Chimera Beats Both Parents

Capability Transfer via Surgical Layer Grafts: EXP6 vs Llama-3.1-8B and R1-Distill-Llama-8B Across MMLU, 500Q, and GSM8K

Trained language models contain capability that is, in principle, movable. The reasoning pathways a model learns are not welded to the rest of its knowledge—they can be transplanted into a different host. This paper documents one of the cleanest empirical demonstrations of that principle we have: a "chimera" model built from two parents that exceeds the capability ceiling of both, on three independent benchmarks, under matched clean-harness conditions.

Surgical model modification produces "chimeras"—models built from layers of multiple parent models. The skeptic's natural question is whether the surgery actually adds capability or just shuffles it around at zero net gain. EXP6—a Llama-3.1-8B host with R1-distilled-Llama-8B layers L18–26 grafted into the terminal-reasoning region—answers the question with measurable margins on MMLU, 500Q, and GSM8K. The deltas are modest. The phenomenon they document is general.

Under matched clean-harness conditions (Q6_K quantization, post-2026-05-01 fixed harness with thinking-tag stripping, deterministic shuffle, all parents tested on identical scripts), EXP6 exceeds both parent models on three independent benchmarks:

  • MMLU (1,995 questions, muzzled): EXP6 62.4% > Llama 59.1% > R1-Distill CoT 52.5%
  • 500Q (505 questions, mixed reasoning): EXP6 84.2% > R1-Distill 83.0% > Llama 80.2%
  • GSM8K-IL67 (1,319 questions, fair prompt): EXP6 85.97% > Llama 80.36% > R1-Distill-Q6 77.26%

Chimera-beats-both-parents is not an interpolation between parents. It exceeds the ceiling of both. The graft adds capability the parents individually cannot produce.

The deltas are modest in absolute terms—the chimera-vs-host MMLU gap is +3.3pp, the chimera-vs-host GSM8K gap is +5.61pt—but they replicate cleanly across independent runs and survive the most rigorous fair-prompt conditions we know how to construct. This paper documents the methodology, the cross-parent picture, and the WildClaw task-swap forensics that exonerated the chimera from an apparent execution-capability regression that turned out to be a harness contamination story.

Benchmark and harness

All measurements use the post-2026-05-01 clean harness pattern: thinking-tag stripping across all four thinking-format families (<think>, <begin_of_thought>, <channel|>, [THINKING]), truncation-guard for budget exhaustion, deterministic per-question shuffle to defeat letter-position artifacts, per-host output paths to prevent SMB last-writer-wins races. All three models tested at Q6_K, temp=0, with identical max_tokens budgets and identical system prompts per benchmark.

Three benchmarks chosen for orthogonal capability axes:

  • MMLU (1,995 questions): Broad-knowledge multiple choice across 57 subjects. Tests calibration and recall.
  • 500Q (505 questions): Mixed reasoning multiple choice from Fireside's internal evaluation set. Tests structured reasoning.
  • GSM8K-IL67 (1,319 questions): Math word problems under explicit permission prompt. Tests multi-step calculation.

The Triangle Inequality: Why This Isn't Interpolation

A naive expectation might be that a chimera lands somewhere between its two parents—a kind of weight-averaged interpolation. The EXP6 data refutes that directly:

Why the Chimera Isn't Just an Average

On MMLU, the host scores 59.1% and the donor scores 52.5% (CoT). An interpolation between them would predict the chimera lands somewhere in the 52.5–59.1% range. The chimera lands at 62.4%—above the higher parent's ceiling. On 500Q, the same pattern: host 80.2%, donor 83.0%, chimera 84.2%—above the higher parent. On GSM8K-IL67, host 80.36%, donor 77.26%, chimera 85.97%—5.6pt above the higher parent. The chimera doesn't just inherit from each parent; the combination produces capability neither parent has alone.

The mechanism is plausible: R1's reasoning-trained layers in the host's terminal region give the host's earlier-layer knowledge structure access to a reasoning pathway it doesn't natively have. The host's broad calibration plus the donor's reasoning depth produces a composite that's strictly better than either component alone on the metrics where both capabilities matter.

This is consistent with the Identity Preservation principle (IL10): grafts on terminal FFN layers preserve the host's identity and calibration while transplanting a specific capability. The reasoning pathway from R1 doesn't overwrite Llama's knowledge—it routes Llama's knowledge through a better inference engine.

The Graft Is Architecturally Real (IL134 Forensic Validation)

Iron Law 134 documents a failure mode where a surgical graft "loads, infers, and benchmarks normally" but produces zero donor signal—cos(chimera, host) at the graft layers indistinguishable from a no-op. Silent graft failures had bitten earlier surgical experiments (the triple_r1-100_coder-10 case showed cos=0.994 at intended graft layers, meaning the donor's R1 weights never actually transferred). Before claiming IL137 on EXP6, we ran the forensic check.

33× the noise floor at the intended graft layers. The R1 donor weights are architecturally present in EXP6's L18–26 with strong signal, not a silent no-op. The IL137 cross-parent advantage is supported by an architecturally real graft, not a coincidence from the surgical script reverting to the host's weights.

This step matters because the broader Foundry experience shows surgical scripts can fail silently—dimension mismatches between donor and host, GGUF metadata pointing at the wrong tensor block, RoPE positional encoding breakage at the graft boundary all produce "chimeras" that benchmark fine but contain zero donor contribution. IL134 forensics are the cosine-similarity verification step we run before publishing any cross-parent claim.

WildClaw Task-Swap Forensics: The Chimera Was Exonerated

A separate scare came from WildClaw English, the multi-step bash workflow benchmark. Historical EXP6 measurements showed 30/60 retry; current measurements showed 17/60—a −13-task drop that looked like the chimera had lost execution capability. The natural hypothesis was that surgery costs WildClaw performance.

The hypothesis was wrong. Thor's forensic audit of the WildClaw task set revealed:

  • 12 tasks were swapped between April 6 and the current harness—the WildClaw task set is not pinned and had evolved silently.
  • 9 of the 12 removed tasks were passes (English code/creative tasks the chimera was reliably solving).
  • The 12 replacement tasks were Chinese/VLM tasks that the chimera reliably fails—a workload the chimera was never built for.
  • Task-swap accounted for −9 of the drop; flips on 48 common tasks accounted for −4. Total: 30 − 13 = 17. ✓
The Exam Changed, Not the Student

The chimera is exonerated. The historical 30/60 → current 17/60 drop was almost entirely a task-set drift artifact, not a model regression. WildClaw is not a stable longitudinal benchmark—the task set must be pinned for valid cross-time comparison. The chimera approach didn't lose capability; the exam changed.

This is itself an iron-law-grade methodology lesson: benchmark identity matters. A score labeled "WildClaw English" can mean different things at different points in time if the task set hasn't been version-pinned. For longitudinal model comparison, the benchmark either needs an explicit version pin or every measurement needs to record which specific task set was used.

The Scope of the IL137 Claim—Honest Caveats

The "chimera beats both parents" claim is specifically and narrowly true. Things it does not claim:

EXP6 is not the best model in its deployment tier

For 8B-class deployments, separate Fireside work shows that Google's gemma-4-E2B at 3GB VRAM scores 90.37% on GSM8K-IL67—beating EXP6 (8GB at Q6) by +4.4pt at less than half the deployment footprint. EXP6's chimera advantage is over its specific parents (Llama-3.1-8B and R1-Distill-Llama-8B), not over the entire 8B-class competitive set. If your deployment question is "what's the best math model at consumer VRAM," the answer is gemma-4-E2B, not EXP6.

The advantage shrinks under fair prompts

An earlier framing claimed EXP6 was +14pt over R1-Distill on GSM8K. That measurement was taken under a system prompt that sandbagged R1-Distill's thinking-mode reasoning. Under fair (IL67-style) prompts, the same gap shrinks to +8.71pt. The chimera advantage is real under both prompts, but the magnitude is honest only at the fair-prompt measurement.

The advantage doesn't scale to 14B

Companion work (the cross-scale ROI inversion paper) shows that grafting the same R1 reasoning capability into a 14B host produces net-negative results. The 14B host (Qwen2.5-14B) already does multi-step calculation competently—there's no headroom for the graft to add capability to, and the calibration cost dominates. The chimera approach works at 8B because the host has headroom. It does not work at 14B+.

The advantage is concentrated on specific problem types

Per-question discriminative analysis (companion paper) shows EXP6's GSM8K wins concentrate sharply on multi-step money/percentage problems where the host truncates calculation partway through. On single-step problems, both models tend to pass; on questions requiring knowledge the host doesn't have, both fail. The +5.61pt headline overstates the chimera's "general capability gain"—the gain is targeted.

Why This Matters

It demonstrates that trained capability is movable

The triangle inequality—chimera 62.4% > host 59.1% > donor 52.5% on MMLU, chimera above both parents on every benchmark measured—is not a benchmark-race result. It is evidence that trained capability can be transferred: two networks, each with a ceiling, can be combined via targeted layer grafts to produce a network whose ceiling is higher than either component's. For an enterprise asking "can we add capability to the models we already deploy without retraining from scratch?", this is the empirical foundation of an affirmative answer. The deltas in any single experiment are narrow. The principle they document is general.

It validates the surgical paradigm at small scale

The IL137 result confirms that surgical layer grafts can produce composite models that exceed both parents on benchmarks where neither parent is at ceiling. This isn't free capability creation (per-graft cost work shows the cost shows up elsewhere on stacked chimeras), but it does establish that surgery is positive-sum at the right operating point.

It validates the broader methodology

The IL134 forensic check (33× noise-floor divergence at graft layers) plus the WildClaw task-swap exoneration plus the cross-parent triangle inequality together establish that EXP6's advantage is real and architecturally grounded. The methodology stack—clean harness, thinking-tag stripping, deterministic shuffle, per-host paths, cosine-similarity validation, task-set version pinning—is what lets us make a defensible cross-parent claim. None of the individual checks would be sufficient alone.

It frames the chimera value proposition correctly

EXP6 is the right starting point for a customer conversation about "what can surgical modification do for our model fleet?" The chimera beats both parents by measurable margins, the advantage is mechanistically grounded, and the limits (deployment tier, scale, problem type) are explicit. Practitioners can decide whether the chimera advantage matches their workload—it's a tool with known scope, not a universal upgrade.

What's Next

HumanEval cross-parent measurement

The 8B precision-graft sweep (EXP1–EXP8) under the post-fix harness needs HumanEval data to complete the per-axis capability picture. Code generation is the next benchmark to add to the IL137 evidence stanza. Half-day compute.

Qwen3-8B baseline for 8B-class ceiling

Without Qwen3-8B's clean MMLU/GSM8K numbers under the same harness, we can't fully bound where EXP6 sits in the 8B-class competitive set. Comparison to Gemma-4 family already shows EXP6 is mid-pack on math; Qwen3-8B will close the picture on MMLU.

WildClaw with pinned task set

Re-run EXP6 vs Llama on a version-pinned WildClaw English task set. If the task-swap exoneration is correct, EXP6 should now show stable or modestly improved execution capability versus the host. Without the pin, longitudinal comparisons remain unreliable.

Replicate EXP6's recipe on a non-Llama 8B host

The IL137 result is one recipe on one host. Replicating EXP6's surgical recipe on Qwen3-8B or Mistral-7B as the host would test whether the chimera advantage transfers across base architectures or is Llama-specific. This is the critical generalization test before IL137 gets treated as a general law rather than a single empirical observation.
