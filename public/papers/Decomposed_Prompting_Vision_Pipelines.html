Decomposed Prompting in Vision Pipelines: The Local Advantage

Decomposed Prompting in Vision Pipelines

Model Selection, Prompt Structure, and the Economics of Local vs. Cloud Vision AI

Executive Summary — 30-Second Read
  • Local beats cloud, clearly. Qwen2.5-VL-7B running on your own GPU scores 86.8% on spatial visual reasoning. Gemini 2.5 Flash (paid cloud API) scores 76.8%. That is a 10-point accuracy gap in favor of the local model — and the local model costs zero per inference at scale.
  • At 3 million images per month, the savings are $1,500–$30,000/month depending on prompt design complexity. A cloud API with single-shot prompts runs approximately $1,500/month; with decomposed prompts and ensemble sampling it reaches $30,000/month. A local Qwen-7B deployment runs at zero marginal cost after hardware. The hardware pays for itself in the first month.
  • Newer is not always better. Qwen3-VL-8B is the newer generation of the same family. It scores 3–4 points lower than Qwen2.5-VL-7B on spatial reasoning across all conditions. Generation upgrades are not free accuracy upgrades.
  • How you write the prompt is a production parameter, not an afterthought. Bundling multiple subtasks into one Qwen prompt costs 9 points of accuracy and triples hallucination rate. The prompt structure you use is as consequential as which model you pick.
  • Phrasing "right" vs. "left" gives you 30 free points on laterality questions. Asking "Is the patient's right side visible?" gets 97% accuracy. Asking "Is the patient's left side visible?" gets 67%. Same image. Same model. Same pipeline. Prompt phrasing alone.

The Deployment Decision in Front of You

You are evaluating vision-language models (AI that analyzes images) for a production pipeline. The pipeline might process customer-uploaded photos for fraud detection or quality grading. It might analyze medical imagery for anatomical orientation or clinical attribute extraction. It might run industrial inspection for defects and completeness.

The standard evaluation approach: look at benchmark leaderboards, pick the highest-scoring model, sign a cloud API contract, and integrate.

Here is what that approach misses:

  • The model scoring highest on the generic benchmark may score 10 points lower than a smaller local model on your specific task type.
  • How you structure the prompt determines whether the model hallucinates at a 13% rate or a 33% rate — same model, same images, only the prompt differs.
  • At 3 million images per month, the cloud API that looks affordable at low volume costs $1,500–$30,000/month depending on pipeline design — reaching the high end when you need ensemble accuracy. The local alternative costs zero per inference.
  • A single phrasing choice in your prompt ("right side" vs. "left side") swings accuracy by 30 points on laterality questions.

None of these appear on leaderboards. All of them determine whether your deployment succeeds or fails. This paper covers each one.

The Cost Case: Local vs. Cloud at Scale

Before the accuracy data, the economics. The cost difference at production scale is large enough to be the dominant factor in the decision, independent of accuracy.

Cost Comparison at 3 Million Images / Month

Qwen2.5-VL-7B on your own GPU: $0 per inference. Whether you run each image once or five times for ensemble accuracy, the marginal cost is zero after hardware. One RTX 5090 (~$2,000) handles 50–100 images per second. The hardware pays for itself against a cloud bill in the first month.

Gemini 2.5 Flash (cloud API): $1,500–$30,000/month depending on pipeline design. Single-shot: ~$1,500. Decomposed into 5 calls per image: ~$7,500. Decomposed with 4-sample ensemble for accuracy: ~$30,000. And the local model still outperforms it by 10 points.

Deployment Approach Per-Image Cost 3M Images / Month Accuracy (Spatial Tasks)
Gemini 2.5 Flash — single-shot ~$0.0005 ~$1,500 76.8%
Gemini 2.5 Flash — decomposed (5 calls) ~$0.0025 ~$7,500 76.8% (per call)
Gemini 2.5 Flash — decomposed + 4-sample ensemble ~$0.01 ~$30,000 76.8% (per call)
Local Qwen2.5-VL-7B (BF16) — any pipeline design $0 incremental $0 86.8%

The decomposition tax that costs $7,500 per month on cloud is free locally. The ensemble option that pushes the cloud bill to $30,000 per month is also free locally. And the local model is 10 points more accurate on the task type where this comparison applies: spatial visual reasoning, the backbone of image orientation, quality grading, and anatomy detection.

Cost estimates assume single-shot (low end) to decomposed-plus-ensemble pipelines (high end), at current Gemini Flash 2.5 pricing tiers. Local deployment cost is $0 per inference after capex.

Deploy Now: Recommendations by Use Case

Skip to your use case. The technical evidence supporting each recommendation is in the sections below.

Customer-Uploaded Image Classification
Qwen2.5-VL-7B at BF16 — Structured Single-Call Prompt

Damaged product photos, receipt verification, ID document validation, quality grading. Single RTX 4090 or 5090. Use one structured prompt per image with labeled output fields — not a paragraph-style bundle of questions. Expected accuracy: 83–87%. Hallucination rate under 3%. Cost: zero per inference after hardware.

Medical / Clinical Imagery
Qwen2.5-VL-7B at BF16 — Decomposed Single-Task Prompts, Local Only

Skin assessment, patient orientation, anatomical region detection. Run separate inference calls per attribute (orientation, features, measurements) rather than one bundled call. Always phrase laterality questions as "right" rather than "left" where possible — 30-point accuracy difference. Use containment phrasing ("Is the lesion on the dorsal surface?") over directional phrasing. Local-only for HIPAA compliance. Escalate low-confidence answers to human review.

High-Volume Triage (1M+ Images / Month)
Qwen2.5-VL-7B at BF16 — vLLM Continuous Batching

Production pipelines at scale. One or two RTX 5090s with vLLM continuous batching. Single-task prompt per decision — no bundling. Run each image once at temperature=0. Spot-check 1–5% against the larger Qwen3.6-27B-Vision for quality drift detection. At 3M images/month: API cost (Gemini Flash) ~$7,500/month; local Qwen-7B cost ~$0/month after hardware.

Mixed or Uncontrolled Input Quality
Gemma-4-26B-Vision — Prompt Structure Doesn't Matter

User-typed queries against images, unknown or variable prompt structure at inference time. Gemma-4-26B is the only tested model whose accuracy is flat across all prompt structures. You sacrifice 5–8 points of peak accuracy versus Qwen at clean single-task prompts, but you gain robustness to whatever prompt format reaches the model. When you cannot enforce prompt discipline, Gemma's structural robustness is worth the accuracy trade.

For controlled pipelines: Qwen2.5-VL-7B (BF16) with decomposed prompts. For uncontrolled input: Gemma-4-26B-Vision. For cloud-vs-local decisions at scale: the local model wins on both accuracy and cost.

The Prompt Structure Finding: 9 Points and Triple Hallucination

This is the finding that catches most production deployments by surprise — because it is invisible until the system is live.

The Qwen-VL model family loses approximately 9 percentage points of accuracy when you bundle multiple visual questions into one prompt versus asking each question in isolation. This effect is consistent across every Qwen-VL model size we tested (3B, 7B, and 27B), across quantization levels, and likely across other visual task types beyond spatial reasoning.

Here is what "bundled prompt" means in practice: you ask the model to describe the scene, list objects, assess lighting, identify colors, and answer your spatial question — all in one call. That is the typical production prompt, because it saves inference calls. For Qwen models, it costs 9 points of accuracy and triples the hallucination rate.

Model Single-Task Prompt Structured Call Bundled Multi-Task Accuracy Loss
Qwen2.5-VL-7B (BF16) 86.8% 84.3% 81.1% −5.7pt
Qwen3.6-27B-Vision (Q6) 86.8% 85.2% 77.5% −9.3pt
Qwen3.6-27B-Vision (Q4) 86.6% 83.9% 75.0% −11.6pt
Qwen3-VL-8B (BF16, newer gen) 83.6% 81.1% 77.2% −6.4pt
Qwen2.5-VL-3B (Q6) 81.3% 78.0% 72.6% −8.7pt
Gemma-4-26B-Vision (Q4) 78.6% 79.1% 79.3% +0.7pt (flat)
Gemma-4-31B-Vision (Q4) 74.8% 79.8% 79.8% +5.0pt (improves)
Gemini 2.5 Flash (cloud API) 76.8% 76.3% 78.3% +1.5pt (flat)

N=440 examples from the Visual Spatial Reasoning dataset. Bootstrap 95% CIs on per-model accuracy are ±2–3 percentage points. The 9-point Qwen overload gap is well above this noise floor. All local models served via llama.cpp on a single RTX 5090 at temperature=0. BF16 = full-precision floating point; Q6/Q4 = quantized variants that trade small accuracy for lower VRAM usage.

The Gemma family is completely flat across all three prompt conditions — multi-task bundling costs it nothing. This is an architectural difference between the two families, not a quality difference. It determines which model fits your deployment: if you control the prompt structure (most production pipelines), Qwen at clean single-task prompts is the stronger choice. If you cannot control prompt structure, Gemma's robustness is the right trade.

Why Bundled Prompts Triple Hallucination Rate

Multi-task overload does not degrade Qwen accuracy uniformly. It specifically inflates the hallucination rate (false positive — the model asserting things that aren't there). For Qwen3.6-27B-Vision on the same images: single-task prompt = 13.6% hallucination rate. Bundled multi-task prompt = 33.0% hallucination rate. The miss rate stays flat. Under overload, the model becomes systematically more credulous: when uncertain, it says yes. For medical or safety-critical applications, this distinction between the failure modes matters as much as the headline accuracy.

Newer Is Not Always Better: The Qwen3-VL Finding

The conventional assumption is that a newer model generation outperforms the previous one. On spatial reasoning, this assumption is wrong for the Qwen-VL family.

Qwen3-VL-8B is the current generation, released after Qwen2.5-VL-7B. At matched BF16 precision, it scores 83.6% on single-task spatial reasoning. Qwen2.5-VL-7B scores 86.8%. The older model wins by 3.2 points across all conditions tested.

This is not a defect in Qwen3-VL. It almost certainly represents a capability trade: Qwen3-VL was likely optimized for OCR, multilingual visual content, document understanding, and compositional reasoning — tasks we did not measure in this benchmark. For those tasks, Qwen3-VL may well be stronger. For spatial reasoning, Qwen2.5-VL-7B is the current production champion.

The business implication: before you upgrade model generations in your vision pipeline, measure the tasks you actually care about. A generation upgrade is not a free accuracy upgrade.

Left/Right Asymmetry: 30 Free Accuracy Points from Prompt Phrasing

This finding costs nothing to act on and has immediate impact on any pipeline involving laterality.

Qwen2.5-VL-7B achieves near-perfect accuracy on "right side" spatial questions and significantly lower accuracy on "left side" questions — across the same image distribution, same model, same prompt structure.

Question Phrasing N Accuracy
"Is the X at the right side of the Y?" 30 96.7%
"Is the X at the left side of the Y?" 30 66.7%
"Is the X right of the Y?" 26 100%
"Is the X left of the Y?" 30 86.7%

The mechanism is not fully confirmed, but the practical implication is concrete: whenever your pipeline can be phrased in terms of "right" rather than "left," make that change. For medical applications, "Is the patient's right side facing the camera?" achieves ~97% accuracy, versus ~67% for the left-side phrasing. For a 10,000-image pipeline, that single prompt change eliminates approximately 3,000 errors.

Production Action — Phrasing Rule for Laterality Questions

In any pipeline involving spatial orientation, side detection, or laterality: frame questions in terms of "right" rather than "left" wherever the domain allows. Rephrase "Is the damage on the left panel?" as "Is the damage on the right panel?" by adjusting the downstream logic, not the image analysis. For medical imaging, prefer "patient's right side" framing over "patient's left side" framing. This costs no compute and recovers 30 percentage points of accuracy on the relevant question class.

The Size Scaling Finding: 7B Is Enough for Spatial Reasoning

One of the clearest findings in this benchmark: for spatial reasoning, scaling from 7B to 27B parameters gains 0.2 percentage points. Scaling from 3B to 7B gains 5.3 points.

Model Single-Task Accuracy VRAM (approx.) Accuracy Gain vs. Previous
Qwen2.5-VL-3B (Q6) 81.3% ~4 GB —
Qwen2.5-VL-7B (BF16) 86.8% ~16 GB +5.5pt
Qwen3.6-27B-Vision (Q6) 86.8% ~22 GB +0.0pt

The 27B model is not useless — it substantially outperforms 7B on OCR, multilingual content, complex compositional reasoning, and document understanding. But for spatial-relation classification, orientation detection, and the task class most relevant to image quality grading and anatomical analysis, 7B is the deployment sweet spot. It requires 3× less VRAM, runs faster, fits more concurrent slots per GPU, and produces identical spatial accuracy to the 27B model.

Hallucination vs. Missed Detection: Failure Direction by Model

For safety-critical applications — medical imaging, fraud detection, quality gates — the direction of failure matters as much as the rate. A model that claims to see something that isn't there (hallucination) creates different downstream risk than a model that misses something real.

Model Hallucination Rate Miss Rate Headline Accuracy Failure Profile
Qwen2.5-VL-3B 7.7% (lowest) 29.8% 81.3% Conservative (says NO)
Qwen2.5-VL-7B 10.0% 16.9% 86.6% Balanced
Qwen3.6-27B (Q6) 13.6% 12.8% 86.8% Balanced and tight
Gemma-4-26B 15.8% 26.9% 78.6% Conservative
Gemini 2.5 Flash 17.2% 29.4% 76.8% Conservative
Gemma-4-31B 37.6% (highest) 12.8% 74.8% Aggressive (says YES)

Hallucination rate = false positive rate (model claims to see a spatial relation that isn't there). Miss rate = false negative rate (model says relation is absent when it's present). Single-task condition. N=440.

For medical applications or safety gates: Gemma-4-31B is disqualified by its 37.6% hallucination rate regardless of headline accuracy. A model that asserts the patient's right side is visible when it is not can lead to incorrect downstream decisions before human review catches it. Qwen2.5-VL-7B at 10% hallucination rate and 17% miss rate provides the best balance in the field.

For applications where you need maximum conservatism (minimize false positives, accept more missed detections): Qwen2.5-VL-3B at 7.7% hallucination rate is the cleanest option, at the cost of higher miss rate.

Quantization: How Much Accuracy Does Compression Cost?

Quantization (compressing model weights to reduce VRAM and inference cost) is a practical deployment decision. Here is the cost on Qwen3.6-27B-Vision:

Prompt Condition Q6_K (higher quality) Q4_K_XL (lower VRAM) Accuracy Cost
Single-task (recommended) 86.8% 86.6% −0.2pt (negligible)
Structured single-call 85.2% 83.9% −1.3pt
Multi-task bundled 77.5% 75.0% −2.5pt

For clean single-task prompts, Q4 quantization costs 0.2 points of accuracy. In exchange, the 27B model fits in 16 GB of VRAM instead of 22 GB — enabling deployment on a single consumer GPU (RTX 4090) with room for concurrent inference slots. For the recommended production setup (single-task or structured prompts), Q4 is the right deployment choice. Deploy Q6 only if you cannot control prompt structure.

How We Measured This

We used the Visual Spatial Reasoning (VSR) dataset — 440 examples of natural images paired with spatial-relation questions requiring yes/no answers. We tested seven model configurations under three prompt structures (single-task, structured single-call, and multi-task bundled) to isolate the effect of prompt structure on accuracy.

The three prompt structures tested:

Condition 1 — Single-Task (Recommended for Qwen)
"Look at the image. Is the X at the left side of the Y? Answer with exactly one word: YES or NO."
Condition 2 — Structured Single-Call (Labeled Output)
"Look at the image. Answer each item on its own line:
SCENE: <one sentence description>
OBJECTS: <comma-separated list>
LIGHTING: <one phrase>
COLORS: <one phrase>
SPATIAL: <YES or NO answering — Is the X at the left side of the Y?>"
Condition 3 — Multi-Task Bundle (Typical Production Prompt)
"Look at the image carefully. First, briefly describe the overall scene. Second, list the main objects. Third, describe the lighting. Fourth, describe the dominant colors. Finally, answer this with one word — YES or NO: Is the X at the left side of the Y?"

All three conditions require the same spatial yes/no answer. They differ only in what else the model is simultaneously asked to process. The accuracy difference reveals how much each model's spatial reasoning is disrupted by concurrent tasks.

For object-hallucination characterization, we also ran the POPE benchmark on Qwen2.5-VL-7B (500 questions of the form "Is there a [object] in this image?"), finding a 2.8% hallucination rate and 23.6% miss rate with a strong NO-bias. The low hallucination rate is the production asset; the miss rate can be managed with absence-framing ("Is the object absent?") for high-recall requirements.

Limitations and Honest Caveats

Spatial reasoning only. OCR, counting, attribute extraction, and compositional reasoning are not characterized in this benchmark. The prompt-structure sensitivity finding on Qwen should be independently verified for those task types before being applied to them.

No medical or domain-specific images. VSR uses COCO-derived natural imagery. Accuracy on actual clinical photography, industrial inspection images, or customer-uploaded product photos may differ. The directional findings should hold; the specific accuracy numbers may shift.

Q4 and Q6 quantization only. Q3 and lower quantizations were not tested. Based on the Q6-to-Q4 accuracy trajectory, Q3 would likely degrade accuracy significantly.

Two model families only. We have not tested InternVL, DeepSeek-VL, Pixtral, or MiniCPM-V. The family-level patterns we identify (Qwen overload-sensitive, Gemma overload-robust) may not generalize to those architectures.

N=440 for the main benchmark. Bootstrap 95% confidence intervals on per-model accuracy are ±2–3 percentage points. The 9-point overload gap is well above this noise floor. Smaller per-condition differences should be treated as directional.

CEO Action Items

01
If you are evaluating cloud vision APIs for a pipeline above 500K images/month, run a local Qwen2.5-VL-7B comparison first

The economics invert at scale. A single RTX 5090 (~$2,000) handles this benchmark's task class at 50–100 images/second with 10 points better accuracy than Gemini Flash and zero per-inference cost. The evaluation takes one day. The cost difference at 3M images/month ranges from $1,500 (single-shot cloud) to $30,000 (decomposed plus ensemble cloud) against zero for local inference.

02
Audit your current vision prompts for multi-task bundling

If your production Qwen-VL prompts bundle multiple visual questions into one call, you are operating at 9 points below the model's achievable accuracy. Decomposing into single-task calls is an engineering change, not a model change. On a 1M-image pipeline, that represents approximately 90,000 additional correct answers per month at no additional cost.

03
Change all "left side" phrasing to "right side" phrasing in laterality prompts this week

This is a one-line change in your prompt template and a corresponding logic inversion in downstream processing. It recovers 30 percentage points on laterality questions. If your pipeline processes any images involving side orientation — patient positioning, package labeling, product facing — this is the highest-leverage engineering action available.

04
Before upgrading to a newer model generation, benchmark your specific task

Qwen3-VL-8B is newer than Qwen2.5-VL-7B. It scores 3–4 points lower on spatial reasoning. Model generation upgrades are not free accuracy improvements on all task types. Run your task-specific benchmark before committing to a migration, especially if your current production model is performing well.

05
For safety-critical applications, disqualify Gemma-4-31B-Vision on hallucination rate alone

37.6% false positive rate means the model asserts a spatial relationship is present in more than one-third of cases where it isn't. For medical imaging, orientation detection, or any safety gate, this is a disqualifying failure mode independent of headline accuracy. Qwen2.5-VL-7B at 10% hallucination rate and 17% miss rate is the safe production choice in this field.
