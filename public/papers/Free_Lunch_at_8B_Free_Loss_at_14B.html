Free Lunch at 8B, Free Loss at 14B

An Empirical Cross-Scale Study of Targeted Layer Customization on Small Language Models

The same recipe adds 3.5 points at 8B and costs 9.9 points at 14B. Not a small difference. A complete inversion. We applied an identical targeted layer modification to two host models at different scales—Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen2.5-14B—under a fixed harness across three single-question benchmarks (TruthfulQA, HellaSwag, GSM8K). At one scale the modification is net-positive. At the other, it's a net loss. Here is why, and what it means for anyone scaling targeted customization pipelines upward.

At 8B, the modification costs ~2pt of commonsense calibration and buys 5.6pt of step-by-step math—net +3.5pt. At 14B, the same modification recipe costs 9pt of calibration AND buys roughly 0pt of math, because the 14B host already does math at 95%—net −9.9pt. The math gain that justified the modification at 8B has evaporated by 14B.

We additionally measured multi-step bash workflow performance (WildClaw English, n=3 replicate runs per model). At 14B, the modified variant shows +5.0pt first-try and +4.6pt retry vs the vanilla host—both within or borderline of the harness noise floor (t=1.34 and t=2.08 respectively). There is no axis where the 14B modification clearly wins.

The value of narrow customization is inversely proportional to host capability. The Free Lunch hypothesis at 8B was real and reproducible. It does not generalize to 14B.

This is the cleanest empirical evidence we have for what we'll call the Capability ROI inversion. Practitioners scaling targeted-customization pipelines upward should expect diminishing returns turning into outright losses around the 12–14B class for math-targeted customizations on already-strong instruction-tuned bases.

The Question

It's increasingly common to take an instruction-tuned base model (Llama, Qwen2.5, Gemma) and apply targeted modifications to specific layer blocks—replacing "reasoning" layers with weights from a math-tuned model, or adding LoRA adapters tuned for a specific cognitive style. The published framing is usually "you get the base model's capabilities plus a specific specialization, with no significant trade-off."

Two questions practitioners should ask before adopting this approach:

Question 01
Is there really no trade-off?

If the customized model loses calibration or commonsense capability that the base model had, the picture is more complicated than the published headline suggests.

Question 02
Does the gain scale?

If the customization buys 5pt of math at 8B because the base model is weak at math, but buys 0pt at 14B because the base model is already at ceiling, the customization's value evaporates with scale.

This paper measures both questions empirically. Same modification recipe, two scales, three high-confidence single-question benchmarks.

Setup

The modification we tested is a precision layer graft: replace a specific contiguous block of layers (mid-to-upper transformer blocks, between the model's planning region and its output region) with weights from a reasoning-tuned variant of the same architecture family. Specifically:

Variant Host Modification
EXP6 (8B variant) Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct Layers 18–26 (of 32) replaced by R1-distilled-Llama-8B's layers 18–26
r1-pruned12 (14B variant) Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct Layers 25–33 (of 48) replaced by R1-distilled-Qwen-14B's layers 25–33, plus 12% magnitude prune

Hosts vs modified variants tested under a fixed harness (llama-server, temp=0, no system-prompt nudges except for GSM8K-IL67 where the prompt encourages step-by-step reasoning) on:

  • TruthfulQA mc1—817 questions, deterministic shuffle to defeat the gold-at-index-0 quirk
  • HellaSwag—1,000-question sample, seed=42
  • GSM8K-IL67—1,319 questions with explicit step-by-step system prompt

All four models tested at Q6_K (8B) or Q4_K_M (14B). Single-question benchmark drift across re-runs is ~1pt—well below the magnitudes claimed below.

Methodology Caveat · Graft + Prune Confound at 14B

The 14B variant (r1-pruned12) carries an additional 12% magnitude prune on top of the layer graft, while the 8B variant (EXP6) is graft-only. This is not a clean two-scale comparison of the same recipe; the 14B loss could in principle be driven by the prune rather than the graft. A graft-only 14B control is queued and will be added to this paper before its public launch. The pattern we observe (gain shrinks with host capability) is consistent with prior cross-scale reasoning-graft work and we expect it to survive the control, but the reader should treat the 14B numbers as bounded by this caveat until the control run completes.

Observation 1 — The math gain shrinks with scale

At 8B, the modification adds 5.61pt to GSM8K-IL67—a substantial improvement. At 14B, the modification adds −1.14pt, within the single-question noise floor (~1pt) and effectively zero. The math gain that justified the modification at 8B has evaporated entirely by 14B.

The mechanism is plausible: GSM8K-IL67 is solvable at 95%+ by a strong instruction-tuned 14B base. There's roughly nothing left for a math-tuned graft to add. At 8B the base scores ~80% on GSM8K-IL67, leaving substantial headroom that the graft can fill. The headroom is the value.

Observation 2 — The calibration cost grows with scale

The modification reduces TruthfulQA by 0.0pt at 8B and 5.75pt at 14B. The 14B base is more calibrated (73.19% vs 54.59%), so it has more calibration to lose. Replacing layers with weights tuned for a different objective—reasoning, not calibration—costs more in absolute terms when the host had more calibration to begin with.

A similar pattern on HellaSwag: −2.10pt at 8B, −3.00pt at 14B. The magnitudes are smaller, but the direction matches.

The 13-point swing in net value across scales is the headline finding. The modification is a good trade at 8B and a bad one at 14B, and the inversion happens because both legs of the trade move in unfavorable directions: cost grows, gain shrinks.

Observation 4 — No multi-step rescue at 14B

One might hope that even if single-question capability degrades, the graft's reasoning-style benefits show up on multi-step agent-loop tasks. They don't.

Metric r1-pruned12-14b qwen2.5-14b vanilla Delta t-stat
First-try (mean ± std) 16.7 ± 6.1 11.7 ± 2.1 +5.0pt 1.34

WildClaw English, n=3 replicate runs, --parallel 2 -c 16384 to eliminate per-slot truncation. Both t-stats in noise or borderline.

The 14B modified variant cannot defend the single-question losses by pointing to a multi-step win. The modification is net-negative at 14B on every axis with confident data.

The high first-try std on the modified variant (±6.1) vs vanilla (±2.1) is itself interesting—the modified model's multi-step performance is more variable run-to-run, suggesting less stable task decomposition. This is consistent with the single-question calibration loss (TruthfulQA −5.75pt): the graft has destabilized the host's answer-commitment behavior.

Deployment Context: VRAM Tier Is the Real Competitive Axis

Before drawing practitioner conclusions, it's worth being explicit about the competitive set. Targeted customization doesn't compete against itself in a vacuum—it competes against whatever else fits on the same hardware.

The 8B modified variant fits in ~6–9GB VRAM. In that tier, the relevant comparison isn't just "modified vs vanilla 8B"—it's also "modified 8B vs gemma4-E4B." The E4B model fits in ~4–6GB VRAM (smaller), scores ~72% TruthfulQA vs the modified 8B's ~55%, and averages 35/60 retry vs the modified 8B's 18/60 on WildClaw multi-step (n=3 each).

The Real Consumer-Tier Winner

In the ≤9GB VRAM consumer tier, gemma4-E4B is smaller than the 8B modified variant and beats it by 17pt on multi-step and ~17pt on factual accuracy. The 8B free lunch is real on the terms we measured—but the competitive set is much bigger than vanilla 8B.

At 14B the picture is worse: both 14B modified and 14B vanilla require ~9–10GB VRAM, so they're in the same tier, and the modified variant underperforms the vanilla on confident axes with no recovery on multi-step.

The deployment recommendation

If your deployment budget is ≤9GB VRAM, ship gemma4-E4B unless you have a specific cognitive axis where you know PLE underperforms. If your deployment budget is ≥17GB, gemma-4-26b-moe is now the strongest tested model by a significant margin. Targeted customization on Qwen/Llama bases occupies a narrowing gap between "smaller than it needs to be" and "not competitive at its size."

Why This Matters

1. For practitioners deploying targeted customizations

Validate at the deployment scale AND against the full competitive set at that VRAM tier. A modification recipe that wins vs vanilla 8B may still lose to PLE alternatives that fit on the same or smaller hardware.

2. For the "free lunch" narrative

At 8B, narrow targeted customizations can be net-positive vs their vanilla host—but the headroom for the targeted gain has to exist, and the vanilla host isn't the only competitor. The free lunch isn't an architectural property; it's a scale-dependent property of what's left for the customization to add, on the axes you're targeting.

3. For scaling pipelines

Targeted customization investment scales most efficiently downward—smaller models, larger relative headroom—rather than upward. A pipeline tuned to produce 8B specialized variants will produce more capability per FLOP than the same pipeline at 14B+.

4. For benchmark interpretation

A customization showing +5pt on a benchmark at 8B isn't evidence the customization is +5pt anywhere. The headroom dynamics matter. Reporting customization deltas should specify "at this scale, vs this host, on this metric"—not "this customization recipe is +5pt."

Limitations

One modification recipe per scale

EXP6 at 8B and r1-pruned12 at 14B are different specific recipes—different layer ranges, prune vs no prune. Same family of modification (R1-style reasoning grafted into mid-upper layers of a Qwen/Llama instruction-tuned base) but not identical.

One model family per scale

The 8B host is Llama-3.1; the 14B host is Qwen2.5. Different base-model families, so cross-scale comparison includes architecture-family confounds.

High multi-step variance at n=3

The modified variant's first-try std (±6.1) is 3× the vanilla std (±2.1), making the t-stat for the +5pt gap only 1.34—in noise. This doesn't mean the modified variant wins multi-step; it means we can't confirm it from this data. The confirmed claim is that it doesn't clearly win, and single-question clearly loses.

No 32B or 70B replication

The slope of the cost/gain trade is suggestive of an inversion around 12–14B, but we don't have data above 14B. The specific inversion-scale could vary by base model, modification family, or benchmark.

What's Next

Replicate the 8B finding with a non-Llama base

Test the same modification recipe on Qwen3-8B or Gemma3-8B to disambiguate base-model-family effects from scale effects.

Measure at 32B

Qwen3-32B is currently the best target. We'd predict the inversion is even more pronounced— more existing capability to displace, less headroom for the modification.

Measure intermediate scales (12B, 20B)

Estimate where exactly the inversion happens for a given base-model family. The 8B→14B transition could be a cliff or a smooth slope; we don't have enough data points to tell.

Measure across modification recipes

Is the inversion specific to R1-style reasoning grafts, or general to any narrow customization on a strong host? Targeted code-generation grafts, instruction-following grafts, and style grafts are all candidates for the same study design.

Targeted customization is a scale-dependent gamble, not a free lunch. Validate at the scale you'll deploy at, and against the full competitive set at that VRAM tier.
