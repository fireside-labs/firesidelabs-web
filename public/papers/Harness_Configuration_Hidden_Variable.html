Harness Configuration Is the Hidden Variable

Verbose Thinking, Permission Prompts, and Why Your Benchmark Numbers Aren't What You Think They Are

We fixed the benchmark harness configuration—not the model, not the hardware—and Qwen3.6's HumanEval score moved by 45.7 points. Then we measured the same effect across our 2026 benchmark fleet. The harness is the hidden variable in published model comparisons. Everything else most teams worry about—quantization, surgery, binary version drift—moves scores by single digits. The system prompt and max_tokens ceiling move them by double digits, sometimes triple. Published benchmark tables almost never specify either.

This paper documents two related failure modes: verbose-thinking truncation (some model families need 4–12× the default token budget to be fairly measured) and permission-prompt sensitivity (a single system-prompt phrasing change swings GSM8K scores by up to 16 percentage points). Together they make untracked harness configuration the largest single source of benchmark variance for thinking-trained models.

Comparing models on benchmarks without standardized harness protocols is roughly equivalent to comparing them at different temperatures. Apples to oranges, dressed up as a leaderboard.

The two harness-configuration variables top the list—each one larger than the variables practitioners spend most of their time worrying about. Researchers benchmark across quantization variants and architecture modifications while the harness defaults are silently moving scores by 2–4× the magnitude.

Part I — Verbose Thinking Silently Breaks Standard Harnesses

Some thinking-mode model families emit thinking traces 2–4× longer than typical for the same problem class. Standard benchmark harnesses with max_tokens=2048 defaults silently truncate these models mid-<think> block. After the server-side reasoning-format strip, content is empty. The model is scored as failing—not because it can't solve the problem, but because the harness assumed a token budget appropriate for one architectural style and not another.

Case study: 30 hours of self-correction on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B

In May 2026 we ran Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (DeltaNet linear attention, MoE) through the standard benchmark battery. Initial results suggested the model was bad at math. They weren't real. Successive max_tokens bumps cut the empty-response rate by roughly half each time:

Same model, same problems, same temperature. The "Qwen3.6 is not a math model" framing collapses entirely under proper harness configuration—Qwen3.6 actually beats Qwen3-30B on every measured axis. It's just expensive in tokens-per-question.

Three concurrent harness bugs in two days, all silently penalizing Qwen3.6

Bug 01 — gsm8k_bench.py
max_tokens=2048 hardcoded

Patched to expose --max-tokens CLI flag. Qwen3.6 GSM8K-IL67 jumped 83.93% → 96.74% with --max-tokens 8192.

Bug 02 — math_bench.py
max_tokens=2048 hardcoded

Same patch applied. Qwen3.6 MATH (L3–5) jumped 6.8% → 31.9% → 54.5% → 68.9% as the cap rose 2K → 8K → 16K → 24K. The model needed 24K thinking room to fully measure on hard MATH problems.

Bug 03 — humaneval_bench.py
Markdown-fence regex ate leading indent

extract_function_body had two bugs: (a) markdown-fence regex r'```python\s*' was too greedy and ate the leading 4-space indent of the first body line; (b) auto-indent logic unconditionally added 4 spaces to every line, double-indenting Qwen3.6's already-indented body to 8 spaces and breaking parse. Patched: line-anchored markdown match + already-indented detection. Qwen3.6 HumanEval jumped 28.7% → 74.4% (ties Gemma 31B's muzzled score exactly).

The within-family asymmetry: same family, opposite relationships with thinking

The verbose-thinking pattern in Qwen3.6 is not just "long traces overflow harness budgets." The deeper architecture-level finding is that Qwen3.6 and Qwen3-30B-A3B—same family, same 3B activation count—have opposite relationships with thinking mode:

Model · WildClaw retry (base-48, n=3) Thinking ON Thinking OFF (muzzled) Δ when muzzled
Qwen3-30B-A3B (standard MoE attention) 40.3 ± 1.5 29.9 ± 2.1 −10pt (thinking is load-bearing)
Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (DeltaNet linear attention) truncates / collapses 76.4 ± 0.6 muzzle is native-best

Qwen3.6 (DeltaNet linear attention) appears to have learned to reason inside the forward pass; Qwen3-30B (standard MoE attention) uses its <think> scratch space. "Thinking-mode" isn't a single architectural property—same family, similar activation count, opposite inference styles.

Three Reporting Numbers, Not One

For cross-model thinking-mode comparisons, the right framing is three numbers: (1) matched-muzzled (both --no-thinking) controls for harness config and shows raw forward-pass capability; (2) native-best each model in its preferred config gives the deployment-reality number; (3) config sensitivity itself shows how much each model gains/loses from thinking. Reporting only one of these will mislead.

The Chinese-12 head-to-head holds across all configs—Qwen3.6 muzzled 77.8%, Qwen3-30B at either config 25.0%, a +52.8pt gap with t > 7. Architecture-quality gap, not config artifact.

Part II — Permission Prompts Swing GSM8K by +5 to +16pt

A single system-prompt change—from "Solve the math problem. End with: #### <number>" to "Show your key reasoning steps. You're allowed to think before answering. Solve the math problem step by step, then give your final numeric answer after #### on its own line."— swings GSM8K scores by +5 to +16pt across thinking-trained 8B models. The architecture-monotonic pattern that didn't generalize

Initially the rescue-rate gradient looked clean:

  • Native niche thinking format (Gemma4 <channel|>, R1 <think>): 55–64% rescue
  • Mixed thinking-graft on non-thinking-host (EXP6) / hybrid (Qwen3): 42% rescue
  • Pure non-thinking (Llama-3.1): 25% rescue

We initially proposed this as an iron-law candidate: permission-tax magnitude tracks architecture in a clean monotonic way. Then we ran the same permission-test on MMLU CoT and the pattern dissolved. Gemma4-E4B was flat (−0.3pt). Gemma4-E2B, EXP6, and Llama all gained ~5pt (similar magnitudes, no architectural gradient). The pattern was math-specific.

Withdrawn Claim — Honest Note

We initially overstated this as IL_PENDING_PERMISSION_TAX_BY_ARCH—an architecture-driven iron law. The MMLU cross-benchmark test falsified the architecture-monotonic pattern. The honest finding is narrower: on math-word-problem benchmarks, permission framing helps thinking-trained models, but the magnitude doesn't follow a clean architecture-driven gradient on knowledge tasks. We withdrew the iron-law proposal.

Methodology lesson: rescue-rate projection systematically overshoots

Our initial "rescue rate × failure count" projection methodology assumed IL67 only HELPS. In reality the new prompt also COSTS some originally-passed questions. Net IL67 effect is much smaller than rescue rate suggests, and the magnitude of error correlates with the original rescue rate:

Model Projected Actual (full retest) Projection error
gemma4-E2B93.56%90.37%−3.19pt
EXP691.13%85.97%−5.16pt
R1-Distill-Q687.64%77.26%−10.38pt
llama-3.1-8b85.06%80.36%−4.70pt

Mean error: −5.86pt. R1-Distill had highest rescue (54%) and biggest projection error (−10.4pt). Llama had lowest rescue (25%) and smallest error (−4.7pt). For future permission-tax studies, treat rescue-rate projection as an upper bound, not a point estimate. Or just measure full retest if budget allows.

The Diagnostic Playbook

The recipe we developed across the May 2026 sprint, applicable to any benchmark whose results look suspiciously low for a thinking-mode model:

01

First sanity check. What fraction of failures have empty content in the result JSON? If >5%, suspect truncation. Empty responses are the canary for verbose-thinking budget exhaustion.

02

Confirm with elapsed-time bunching. Measure median elapsed time of empty-response fails. If it bunches near max_tokens / generation_rate (typically max_tokens / 165 seconds for Q4 thinking models), the model is hitting the cap mid-thinking, not failing on the problem itself.

03

Bump and observe. Double max_tokens and re-run. Each doubling cuts empty rate by roughly half—diminishing returns suggest the verbosity-budget gap is closing. Plot score vs log(max_tokens) to find the inflection.

04

Partial recovery without full re-run. gsm8k_retry_failed.py (template, adapt for math/humaneval) loads existing JSON, retries only fails with bumped budget, merges back. ~80% wall-time savings.

05

VRAM accounting. max_tokens > 12K typically requires bumping per-slot context. Use --ctx (max_tokens × 2.5) --workers (vram-budget // model-size) rule of thumb.

06

Test prompt variants if math scores still look low. Explicit permission framing ("Show your key reasoning steps. You're allowed to think before answering.") can rescue 25–64% of originally-failed math questions for thinking-trained models. Generic "show your work" doesn't reliably trigger native thinking formats.

Practitioner Takeaways

1. Don't trust single-config benchmark numbers across model families

Different architectures emit different thinking-trace lengths. A score that looks low may just be a budget mismatch. Cross-family comparisons require either matched configuration controls or per-model harness tuning—and the methodology should be reported alongside the number.

2. max_tokens=2048 is no longer a safe default

For thinking-mode benchmarks, bump to 8192 for routine measurement; 16K–24K for verbose-thinking variants like DeltaNet-based models. The bigger budget costs wall time but protects against silent truncation contamination.

3. Empty-response rate is the canary

Surface it in benchmark output JSONs as a standard field. The Qwen3.6 case study showed that pre-existing low scores were almost entirely accounted for by empty-content responses—a symptom that any harness can detect cheaply and that no harness currently logs by default.

4. Harness assumptions encode prior models' formatting conventions

Each new model family deserves a sanity-check pass on extraction logic before scores are believed. The HumanEval indent bug only triggered for models that emit ready-to-drop-in code; it had been silent for years against models that emit raw unindented bodies.

5. The cost-per-problem axis matters as much as score

Qwen3.6 is broadly stronger than Qwen3-30B but burns ~3–4× tokens per problem. For latency-sensitive deployments, that ratio dominates the architecture-quality argument. The honest reporting unit may be tokens-per-correct-answer, not raw accuracy.

6. Standardized prompts before vendor comparisons

A model reported as "70% on GSM8K" may score 85%+ with a different system prompt. Comparing models on benchmarks without standardized prompt protocols is the prompt-equivalent of comparing them at different temperatures. Until prompt protocols are standardized in published evaluations, cross-vendor comparisons of thinking-trained models should be treated as directional, not definitive.

What's Next

Generalize the verbose-thinking survey

Which other thinking-mode model families exhibit verbose-thinking? Likely candidates: Llama-3-thought-aware variants, additional R1 variants, DeepSeek-V3 reasoning. Worth a survey-style benchmark across thinking-mode families with empty-rate as the primary metric.

Quantify tokens-per-correct-answer as a unified cost metric

Tonight's data suggests this is a useful metric for cost-aware benchmarking. A model scoring 70% at 500 tokens/answer may be more useful than a model scoring 80% at 2,500 tokens/answer for many production deployments.

Standardize the harness audit as a CI check

Warn on per-slot context < 4K when thinking models are detected. Warn on max_tokens < 4K when measuring math benchmarks. Flag empty-content rate >5% as a likely truncation signal. These are cheap checks that would prevent the most common silent-contamination class.

Within-family asymmetry replication

Test Qwen3.5-35B-A3B as a version-diff to discriminate "DeltaNet causes the asymmetry" from "training-pipeline shapes inference style." Test other "newer version of older family" pairs (Llama-3.5 vs Llama-3, Gemma-3n vs Gemma-3) for parallel asymmetries.

Harness configuration is the largest single variable in published benchmark scores for thinking-trained models. Larger than quantization, surgery, or binary drift. Report your harness, or your numbers are noise.
