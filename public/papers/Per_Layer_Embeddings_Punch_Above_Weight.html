A 3GB Model That Beats Your 14B Workstation AI

A 2-Billion-Effective-Parameter Model Beats 14-Billion Vanilla Dense Models on Multi-Step Bash Workflows at One-Third the VRAM

Per-Layer Embeddings (PLE) is an architectural choice in Google's Gemma 3n / Gemma 4 family that keeps embedding-related parameters resident on CPU and streams only the active per-layer subset to GPU per token. The smallest tested PLE model—gemma-4-E2B ("Effective 2B", 3.8GB GGUF, ~3GB resident VRAM at Q6_K)—was tested under a fixed multi-step bash workflow harness (WildClaw English, n=3 same-config replicate runs at temp=0).

It scored 22.0 ± 1.0 first-try and 31.7 ± 1.5 with-retry out of 60 tasks. Comparing to other tested models at deployment-realistic quantization:

  • Statistically tied with gemma-4-E4B (4B effective, ~6GB VRAM) on retry; loses first-try by ~6pt at 2σ.
  • Beats Qwen3-30B-A3B (3B-activated MoE, ~17GB VRAM) on first-try by 10.3pt (t=3.7) and ties on retry.
  • Beats r1-pruned12-14b (14B modified dense, ~9GB VRAM) on retry by 9.4pt (t=4.6).
  • Dominates qwen2.5-14B-vanilla (~9GB VRAM) by 14 retry tasks (t=9.4, overwhelming).

At ~3GB VRAM—runs on consumer cards including mobile-class hardware—the 2B-effective PLE model is competitive with workstation-class 17GB MoEs on multi-step bash workflows. Bigger model is not better.

The caveat is severe: E2B is narrow. On knowledge-dependent benchmarks (TruthfulQA 53.7%, HellaSwag 60.6%, MMLU 61.6%) it sits well below 8B vanilla baselines. E2B is a math/code/agent-loop deployment artifact, not a general chatbot. This paper is about deployment-decision guidance for the consumer-VRAM tier and what PLE's empirical strength on multi-step says about architecture choice for agent-style workloads.

The pattern is sharp. E2B is strong on execution (math, multi-step bash) and weak on knowledge (TruthfulQA, HellaSwag, MMLU). At 2B effective compute, the model has just enough capacity to learn step-by-step procedural reasoning but not enough to pack broad world knowledge. The execution capability is anomalous given the parameter count; the knowledge capability is roughly what you would predict from compute scaling.

Findings

Capability-per-VRAM is dramatically non-monotonic

The naive deployment heuristic "bigger model = better performance" fails badly here. A 3GB PLE model (E2B) outperforms 9GB dense models on multi-step bash workflows by 9–14 retry points. It even outperforms a 17GB MoE (Qwen3-30B-A3B) on first-try success. Per-token activated compute, not stored parameters, is what determines multi-step capability—and PLE is unusually efficient at packing capability into its activated compute budget for this task class.

E2B is suitable for narrow deployment scenarios

The win is conditional. On knowledge benchmarks (TruthfulQA, HellaSwag, MMLU), E2B sits near the bottom of the fleet—well below 8B vanilla baselines. E2B is not a universal replacement.

Well-suited for:

  • Agent-loop tasks—tool use, bash workflow orchestration, code generation against well-defined APIs
  • Math reasoning under tight VRAM budgets—phone-side coding assistants, edge math reasoning
  • Multi-step task decomposition where the prompt provides the knowledge and the model executes against it

Poorly suited for:

  • Knowledge-RAG-free chatbots—needs a bigger model or RAG augmentation to compensate for the weak knowledge axis
  • General-purpose reasoning where world knowledge is the bottleneck (TruthfulQA-style calibration)
  • Long-form coherent writing where commonsense matters (HellaSwag-style scenarios)

Per-Layer Embeddings Deserve More Attention

PLE's nominal value proposition is memory efficiency—keep half the parameters on CPU and only stream the active embeddings to GPU per layer. The empirical surprise is that PLE produces a model that's not just memory-efficient but unexpectedly strong at multi-step tasks. Two candidate hypotheses:

Hypothesis 01
PLE forces GPU-resident parameters to specialize in computation rather than knowledge storage

The on-CPU embeddings hold lookup-style knowledge; the on-GPU FFN/attention have to learn procedure. For multi-step tasks—which are ~90% procedure, ~10% lookup—this allocation works well. The single-question knowledge benchmark deficits are consistent with this: knowledge sits on CPU and must be streamed, which is a slower path.

Hypothesis 02
PLE's smaller GPU footprint allows larger context per slot

At the same VRAM budget, PLE indirectly helps multi-step by giving the model more room to reason. We did not directly test this; it is a hypothesis. For the runs reported here, all models had identical context budgets, so this can only be a partial explanation.

Either way, the empirical fact stands: at the smallest deployment tier, PLE punches above its compute weight on multi-step. This deserves further architectural investigation—and, in the meantime, deserves practitioner attention when selecting models for agent workloads at consumer VRAM levels.

Practical Guidance for AI Deployment Decisions

When choosing a model for client deliverables, the deployment-cost vs capability trade-off is non-trivial. This table summarizes our findings:

Use case Recommendation VRAM Why
Agent-loop / tool-use / multi-step bash gemma-4-E2B 3GB Dominates 9–17GB models at 1/3–1/6 the VRAM cost
Phone/edge math assistant gemma-4-E2B 3GB 90.7% GSM8K-IL67 at 3GB is exceptional
General chatbot, no RAG gemma-4-E4B or larger 6GB+ E2B's knowledge axis (TruthfulQA 54%) is too weak
Workstation agent loops, max capability gemma-4-26B-A4B 17GB Best-in-fleet on multi-step (78% retry)
Cost-sensitive RAG-augmented chatbot gemma-4-E4B 6GB Best balance of knowledge + capability per GB
Heavy code generation against uncommon libraries Undetermined varies Need code-specific benchmarks beyond WildClaw scope
The Headline Practitioner Finding

For agent-loop workloads at consumer VRAM, the 3GB PLE model beats every dense 14B and ties or beats the 17GB MoE—at one-third to one-sixth the memory footprint. The deployment decision isn't "which is best in the abstract"; it's "which axis matters for my task." If the answer is execution, ship E2B. If it's knowledge, ship something bigger.

Limitations

n=3 multi-step but n=1 single-question for E2B

Single-question benchmarks have ~1pt cross-run drift so n=1 is acceptable for those numbers; full transparency is that the WildClaw multi-step numbers are the only ones with explicit confidence intervals here.

One PLE family tested

Gemma 3n / Gemma 4 are currently the only public PLE-architecture models. Our conclusions apply to this family; whether other PLE designs (if released) would show the same multi-step strength is an open question.

WildClaw is one harness

A second multi-step benchmark (SWE-bench Lite, GAIA, agent-tool-use sweeps) would test whether the PLE-punches-above-weight phenomenon survives different long-prompt regimes.

Mixed quantization across the comparison

E2B at Q6_K vs Q4_K_M for the 14B+ comparison models. Q4 may slightly depress capability. The direction of the E2B-wins claim is robust to this gap—dense+PLE at Q6 still beats Q4 14B by 9–14pt across two metrics, an effect size much larger than typical Q4-vs-Q6 drift.

Knowledge-axis underperformance is severe

This isn't a "small concern"—it's a real deployment limitation. If a client task touches general world knowledge, E2B is the wrong choice.

Mechanism is empirically strong but not yet explained

Two candidate hypotheses are presented above but not yet directly tested. Companion architectural investigation is warranted.

What's Next

Map deployment recommendations across more client scenarios

What happens on creative writing? Code review? Multi-document RAG? Long-context summarization? Build a richer benchmark matrix to guide model selection across deployment tiers.

Test PLE at larger scales

If a higher-tier Gemma PLE variant releases (e.g., E16B or larger) and preserves the multi-step efficiency, the consumer-tier story extends to a workstation-tier story. Pending model availability.

Cross-scale comparison work to 32B/70B

This paper covers the 2–17GB tier. The 70B-class story (Llama 3.3 70B, Qwen 2.5 72B at Q4) is the next deployment tier above this. Knowing where E4B / 26B-MoE / 70B sit relative to each other on multi-step would complete the deployment matrix.

Mechanism investigation

Direct test of the "PLE forces compute-vs-knowledge specialization" hypothesis: ablate PLE on a controlled training run, or compare hidden-state norms between PLE and matched-compute dense.

Capability-per-VRAM is non-monotonic. For agent workloads at consumer VRAM, a 3GB PLE model outperforms 9GB dense and 17GB MoE. Pick by task axis, not by parameter count.
