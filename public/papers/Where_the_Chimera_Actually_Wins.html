Where the Chimera Actually Wins

Locating R1-Graft Contribution to Multi-Step Math via Per-Question Discriminative Analysis

The EXP6 chimera (Llama-3.1-8B with R1-distilled-Llama-8B layers L18–26 grafted in) beats its Llama-3.1-8B host by +5.61pt on GSM8K under matched fair conditions (full IL67 permission prompt, all 1,319 questions, Q6_K quantization, fixed harness). The headline number is modest but real. The interesting question isn't "does the chimera win?"—it's where it wins, and what that tells us about which capability the R1 graft actually transferred.

This paper presents a per-question discriminative analysis. We classify all 1,319 GSM8K questions into four outcome buckets (both pass / both fail / EXP6 only / Llama only) and then characterize the 144 EXP6-only wins. The wins concentrate sharply on multi-step money/percentage problems where Llama truncates calculation partway through and EXP6 carries it to completion.

The chimera doesn't make Llama better at single-step problems. It makes Llama better at the questions that require holding intermediate state across multiple computation steps.

This is a useful narrowing of the chimera value claim. Surgery doesn't add diffuse capability across all problems; it adds a specific capability at a specific point in the reasoning pipeline. That capability is locatable, mechanism-consistent with R1's training objective, and small enough that practitioners should treat the chimera as a targeted tool, not a universal upgrade.

The Headline Numbers

Under matched fair conditions—identical IL67 permission prompt, identical harness, identical temperature=0, identical quant—the three relevant models on full 1,319-question GSM8K:

Model Recipe GSM8K-IL67 vs EXP6
EXP6 chimera Llama-3.1-8B + R1-distill L18–26 graft 85.97% —
Llama-3.1-8B (host) baseline 80.36% +5.61pt
R1-Distill-Llama-8B (donor) baseline 77.26% +8.71pt

Both deltas measured under fair conditions—no projections, no rescue-rate extrapolation. The chimera beats both parents on math word problems. The advantage is small but cleanly reproducible.

Context: Smaller-Model Comparison

For honest framing, the chimera's parents are not the strongest math model in its deployment tier. Gemma-4-E2B at 3GB VRAM scores 90.37% on the same benchmark—beating EXP6 (8GB VRAM at Q6) by +4.4pt at less than half the deployment footprint. The chimera claim is "EXP6 beats its specific parents"—not "EXP6 is the best 8B-class math model."

Qualitative inspection of the EXP6-only wins reveals a consistent failure mode in the host model. In each case, Llama's answer is recognizable as an intermediate value from inside the calculation chain. 000 is the last three digits of 130,000 (the pre-profit total). 50 is half of 100, likely from a percentage step. 500 is the trailing three digits of 57,500 after the model's output buffer got confused about which intermediate to commit to.

EXP6 doesn't make these errors. The R1-graft contribution is concrete and locatable: calculation continuity across multi-step word problems where the host loses track partway through.

Mechanism: What the R1 Graft Actually Contributes

The R1-Distill donor was trained on chain-of-thought math reasoning. Its core training signal was producing correct intermediate steps and carrying them through to a final committed answer. When we graft R1's L18–26 into the Llama host, we're transplanting the layers most directly shaped by that training objective.

The empirical observation matches the training story precisely. The graft adds the ability to track running totals across multiple computation steps—exactly what R1's training was designed to add. EXP6's advantage isn't magical; it's the targeted transfer of a specific capability that exists in the donor and didn't exist in the host.

Why This Framing Matters for Surgery Practitioners

The R1 graft has a known scope of useful contribution: multi-step money/percentage word problems. Outside this scope, the chimera shows minimal advantage. This is consistent with the broader Iron Law that narrower grafts leave headroom for stacking, and that composite chimeras (multiple narrow grafts) outperform monolithic ones (one dominant graft). EXP6 is a single-organ chimera with one specialty. Multi-organ chimeras should show wider but smaller advantages across more dimensions.

The Cost: 70 Llama-Only Losses Don't Cluster on Anything

The 70 questions where EXP6 fails but Llama passes are the surgical-damage column of the ledger. We tagged the same features and looked for over-representation:

  • Money: 32% (vs 30% baseline)—within noise
  • Multi-step: 47% (vs 45% baseline)—within noise
  • Percentage: 14% (vs 12% baseline)—within noise
  • Time-related: 58.6% (vs 42% baseline)—modest skew, only feature outside noise

No strong clustering on any feature. The Llama-only wins look like surgical-damage noise—the kind of small, distributed capability loss you'd expect when transplanting one model's weights into another. Net effect: the +144 advantage on money/multi-step/percent more than compensates for the −70 distributed cost.

What This Means for Chimera Practitioners

Surgery adds specific capabilities, not diffuse ones

If you graft R1 layers, you're getting calculation continuity on multi-step word problems. You're not getting a "smarter model" in some general sense. The capability transfers in a narrow, mechanism-consistent way.

The headline accuracy number understates the chimera's specificity

"EXP6 is +5.6pt over Llama on GSM8K" is technically correct but misses the structure. The chimera wins 10.9% of questions, loses 5.3%, and is neutral on the other 83.8%. The wins are concentrated. Practitioners deploying for math workflows where the workload skews toward money/percentage/multi-step problems will see chimera advantages much larger than 5.6pt; workloads that skew elsewhere will see almost none.

Choose the donor for the capability axis you actually need

R1's strength is calculation continuity. A different donor (Coder for code execution, domain-tuned for technical jargon) would produce a chimera with a different concentration of wins on a different problem class. Surgery is a targeting tool, not a generic upgrade.

Single-organ chimeras have known limits

The 144-question win zone is real but bounded by the donor's training-set specialty. Multi-organ chimeras (e.g., R1 graft for math + Coder graft for execution) should produce wider win zones at smaller per-axis magnitudes. The composability story sits in companion work on per-graft costs.

Limitations

Single chimera recipe

EXP6 is one specific surgical recipe (Llama-3.1-8B + R1-distill L18–26 graft). The "R1 graft adds calculation continuity" claim generalizes mechanistically but the specific 144-question win profile is recipe-specific. Different graft positions would produce different win profiles.

Single benchmark

GSM8K is one math benchmark. The "money/percentage/multi-step" win profile is the GSM8K distribution speaking back. A math benchmark with different problem skew would show different concentrations.

8B scale only

We measured at 8B. At 14B, the same kind of graft is net-negative across single-question axes (cross-scale ROI inversion documented separately). The 144-question win zone may shrink or disappear at larger scales where the host already does multi-step calculation competently.

Qualitative failure-mode characterization, not mechanistic proof

We characterize the Llama-truncates-Llama-picks-intermediate pattern by reading question outputs. Mechanistic proof (probe activations on multi-step questions, ablation studies) is future work. The training-objective interpretation is consistent with the data but not yet directly demonstrated.

What's Next

Replicate on 14B chimeras

Does the multi-step money/percent advantage scale, or is it specific to the 8B host? Companion work shows 14B chimeras are net-negative on single-question math—but the discriminative analysis may reveal preserved wins on the same problem class, just offset by larger calibration costs elsewhere.

Test on adversarial multi-step questions outside GSM8K

Build a small benchmark of multi-step money/percent problems that aren't in GSM8K's training contamination zone. If EXP6's advantage persists, the calculation-continuity claim generalizes. If it doesn't, GSM8K's contamination is partially driving the result.

Mechanistic verification

Probe activations at the graft boundary on multi-step versus single-step questions. If R1's L18–26 layers are firing differently on the questions where the chimera wins versus the questions where it doesn't, the calculation-continuity interpretation gets direct empirical support.

Cross-donor comparison

Run the same discriminative analysis on a Coder-grafted chimera, an Instruct-grafted chimera, and a domain-grafted chimera. If each donor produces a different concentration of wins on a different problem class, the targeted-transfer story holds. If all donors produce similar win profiles, something else (general capacity, regularization) is driving the gains.

The chimera advantage is small but mechanistic. R1 grafts add calculation continuity to the host. Pick a donor for the capability axis you need, then measure where the wins actually land.
